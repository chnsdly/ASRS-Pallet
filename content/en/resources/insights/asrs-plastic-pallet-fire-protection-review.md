**Before introducing plastic pallets into an automated warehouse, ask the site's fire protection specialist to review both the loaded storage arrangement and the locations where empty pallets accumulate.** Provide the actual pallet configuration, goods and packaging, rack arrangement and maximum planned storage conditions. An equipment fit check or an HDPE material description does not establish that the existing fire protection design covers the change.

This is a purchasing and project-coordination guide, not a sprinkler design or legal compliance assessment. The responsible specialist should determine the applicable local requirements, adopted standards, insurer conditions and any necessary design or approval changes. Continue to use the [AS/RS pallet selection guide]({{< relref "resources/insights/asrs-plastic-pallet-selection-guide.md" >}}) for the separate mechanical and load checks.

## Why a pallet substitution needs review {#change}

Replacing a wooden pallet with plastic changes a component of the stored unit, even when footprint, goods and rack locations stay the same. Do not assume that the existing protection is automatically adequate or inadequate: the answer depends on the complete storage configuration and the criteria applicable to that facility. A drawing and project-specific review are needed before a purchasing decision is treated as a release for use.

[FM's Data Sheet 8-9, Storage of Class 1, 2, 3, 4 and Plastic Commodities](https://www.fm.com/FMAApi/data/ApprovalStandardsDownload?isGated=false&itemId=%7B78844FB1-B5A2-4877-ABF1-524B8F942611%7D) illustrates how storage arrangements and protection options must be considered together. It is FM property loss prevention guidance, not by itself a law in every jurisdiction. Ask the project specialist which edition and provisions apply to your facility; do not select a sprinkler scheme from an article or a supplier's catalogue.

## Map two different situations {#arrangements}

| Situation | Information to give the fire protection reviewer |
| --- | --- |
| **Loaded pallets in storage** | Goods and packaging, pallet family/model/configuration and material, finished-unit dimensions, storage height, rack and shelf configuration, spacing and the existing protection design. Show any wrap, covers or solid components that could be relevant to the review. |
| **Empty or idle pallets** | Actual locations, maximum anticipated quantities, stacking arrangement and height, whether indoors or outdoors, adjacent goods and equipment, and any time spent in staging or return areas. |
| **Changeover or peak operation** | Where old and new pallet types coexist, temporary staging locations and the maximum simultaneous inventory, not only the average daily quantity. |

Treat temporary staging and empty returns as part of the plan rather than assuming that they inherit the loaded-rack decision. FM publishes a separate [Idle Pallet Storage Data Sheet 8-24](https://www.fm.com/FMAApi/data/ApprovalStandardsDownload?isGated=false&itemId=%7B8E18FAFC-8777-465F-9B96-C46A2273DBF2%7D), illustrating that idle storage arrangements have their own review. The accessible document reviewed here is the January 2015 interim revision; the specialist should obtain the edition applicable to the project. Its particular recommendations must not be copied as universal stack heights or separation distances.

## Prepare a before-and-after record {#record}

Give the reviewer one marked site plan and the following information for both the existing and proposed situation. If a field is unknown, mark it **to confirm** and identify who can supply it.

1. **Pallet:** manufacturer, series, model, configuration and drawing revision; material information actually supplied for that configuration, plus available identification and supporting documents.
2. **Loaded unit:** goods, packaging and wrapping, maximum assembled dimensions, storage orientation and the range of different loads using the pallet.
3. **Rack and building:** rack type, storage height, shelf or support arrangement, aisle and other spaces shown on the existing approved plans, ceiling and any known obstructions.
4. **Empty pallets:** all planned accumulation points, maximum quantities and stack arrangements, including receiving, outfeed, returns and transition stock.
5. **Existing protection:** the latest available approved design, any changes since approval and the protection system owner's records. The fire specialist determines which technical details are needed.
6. **Project boundary:** site location, intended start date, conversion phases and the responsible site, equipment, fire protection and insurance contacts.

The [unit-load dimensions guide]({{< relref "resources/insights/asrs-pallet-unit-load-dimensions.md" >}}) helps prepare the assembled-load envelope. A smaller footprint or an open deck alone does not establish how the complete configuration should be classified or protected.

## Ask for evidence without assuming a listing {#evidence}

Ask the pallet supplier to identify the exact offered configuration and provide any fire-related documentation **that is available and applicable**. If a project requires a particular listing, approval or test, the responsible reviewer should check its issuer, scope, validity and the exact product and configuration covered, using the issuer's current listing where applicable. A general material data sheet, a photograph or a document for another manufacturer's pallet is not evidence that the offered pallet has that listing.

FM Data Sheet 8-24 distinguishes its defined FM Approved pallet category from other pallets for its own recommendations. That is a source-specific distinction, not a statement that a BAOHENG pallet has an FM Approval or a particular fire classification. Where a required record cannot be supplied or verified, leave it unresolved and ask the site reviewer what evidence or alternative is acceptable; do not infer an approval from a similar shape or material.

## Make the release decision together {#decision}

The site owner and fire protection specialist should document the applicable design basis, whether the proposed loaded and empty-pallet arrangements are covered, any required changes and who approves them. The equipment provider and pallet supplier should separately review the interfaces and load conditions; an engineering sample trial does not replace the fire review, and a fire review does not approve the mechanical route.

If the arrangement is outside the reviewed basis or a necessary approval is unresolved, keep that option pending until the responsible parties agree the next step. Do not change sprinkler layouts, bypass protection or increase storage quantities on the basis of this guide. Reassess the decision if pallet material or configuration, packaging, storage height, rack arrangement, idle inventory or protection changes.
