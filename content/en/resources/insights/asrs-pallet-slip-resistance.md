**Start by identifying what is moving against what:** goods against the pallet deck, the pallet against handling equipment, or one empty pallet against another. Then review the actual surfaces, load, environment and route with the responsible pallet, packaging and equipment suppliers. An anti-slip feature on one face does not establish performance at another interface or replace load restraint.

The [stretch-wrap guide]({{< relref "resources/insights/asrs-pallet-stretch-wrap-clearance.md" >}}) covers film anchoring and machine clearance. This guide addresses the separate decision of which slip interfaces and proposed surface treatments need evidence and a representative trial.

## 1. Locate the interface {#interfaces}

| Interface | What to describe | What a change does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goods against deck | Package base, contact area, pallet surface, load pattern and restraint | A grip feature does not prove the entire load is secured. |
| Between package layers | Package materials, any interlayer and wrapping method | Deck friction alone does not prevent layers shifting. |
| Pallet against forks, rollers or chains | Actual contact surfaces, orientation, pickup and transfer sequence | A top-deck feature does not prove adequate equipment contact. |
| Empty pallet against empty pallet | Stack orientation, contact areas, condition and replenishment method | A stable loaded unit does not prove an empty stack is stable. |

[HSE's Pallet safety guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/pm15.pdf), fourth edition, page 4, notes that plastic pallet surfaces can be slippery and that additional measures may be needed for goods, empty stacks and forklift handling. Assess the actual site rather than attributing every movement to the pallet material.

## 2. Record conditions before changing the design {#conditions}

Identify the station, travel direction and point at which movement appears: acceleration, stopping, transfer, pickup or storage. At a designated safe location, record a photo of the finished load, reference marks, observed relative movement and relevant equipment event. Compare good and problematic units. Do not enter a running machine, defeat guarding or adjust a sensor merely to pass the load.

Record the proposed pallet family, model, drawing revision and surface condition; package material and bottom contact; payload and complete unit mass; wrap or straps; and whether surfaces are dry, wet, dusty, cold or affected by the actual cleaning process. The parties should decide which conditions and variations are relevant. Include returning pallets where wear, damage or residue may matter.

The [Virginia Tech pallet testing laboratory](https://www.unitload.vt.edu/facilities/pallet-testing-lab.html) describes separate friction assessments for pallet-to-box, empty-pallet-to-pallet and fork-to-pallet contacts. That illustrates why the tested pair and conditions matter; it does not give a coefficient for a BAOHENG product.

## 3. Compare measures against their actual contact {#measures}

An available deck insert, surface treatment, anti-slip sheet, packaging change or restraint arrangement may be considered for a specific contact. Confirm with the supplier whether the proposed option exists for the exact configuration, where it contacts the load and how it is retained. Do not assume a catalogue picture proves a feature is fitted or available.

An interlayer may change contact at both its top and underside. Review its placement, attachment, wear, cleaning compatibility, exposure to temperature and effect on load stability. An underside feature may interact differently with a roller, chain or fork. Changes can also affect stack height, clearances, sensors, cleaning and the complete unit profile. Ask the system and packaging owners to evaluate these effects before accepting an option.

A friction test is evidence for its stated surfaces and conditions. It is not proof of a universal coefficient, a complete load-restraint design or successful operation through every station. Request the method, sample identity, contact pair, surface condition and results; do not transplant a number from another product or assume a wet condition behaves like a dry one.

## 4. Agree an operational trial and decision {#trial}

With the equipment and packaging owners, define a representative pallet configuration, goods, load pattern, restraint, route and relevant surface conditions. Agree the stations, movements, sample quantity, repetitions and acceptance criteria in advance. Include the empty stack and handling route if relevant. Record what moved relative to what, the observed result and any damage or detection exception.

If unexpected movement, instability or interference occurs, stop and hold the affected unit under the site's procedure and refer it to the responsible reviewers. Keep untested conditions pending. The [sample acceptance checklist]({{< relref "resources/insights/asrs-pallet-sample-testing-checklist.md" >}}) connects this trial to the broader rack and equipment review; a successful slip trial does not establish rack capacity or long-term performance.
